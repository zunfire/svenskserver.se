Rules

  1. No cheating. X-ray, fly hacks, autoclickers and the like get you banned. The server notices more than you think.
  2. Protect what you want to keep. Anything outside protected land is fair game.
  3. The night allows PvP. When darkness falls other players may turn on you, so seek shelter in your home.
  4. Be decent in chat. No threats, no hate and no spam.
  5. Don't lag the server. No gigantic farms or redstone machines running all the time. Ask if you're unsure.
  6. The admin and Smeden have the final word.

Break the rules and Smeden will know. You may end up in the jail … or banned.

Death has a price

When you die you wake up in the nearest jail, every time. There is one at spawn, and more can be built out in the world. Beds and respawn anchors won't help. If you die in the Nether or the End you end up in the jail at spawn. What you carried stays where you fell, but some of it is lost forever and your finest tools are damaged. You also wake up without full health and with little food in your belly. You can walk out of the cell right away.

Then it's a long walk back, without armour and without tools, through a world where the monsters are tougher than you're used to. That is the punishment. Think twice before taking that shortcut over the lava.

Tip: the closer you live to a jail, the shorter the walk home after death. Live far away if you dare.

Nobody gets in alone

New players start at the prison gate. You can walk around and talk in chat, but you can't touch anything: no doors, chests or blocks.

There is no timer that lets you out, and no command. The way in goes through the people on the server: say hello in chat and tell us why you want to play here. If a trusted player believes you, they will come into the cell and strike you. Then you are free.

For your first hour of play after you are let in you are protected: nobody can hurt you in PvP, and you can't use TNT, lava or fire. Just enough to place your chest and build a first house.

The villages are protected

Trade is a big part of life here. Villagers take emeralds, and it pays to travel between villages to buy and sell.

That is why villagers are under special protection. Monsters barely hurt them, and they can't be lured into boats or minecarts.

Whoever murders a villager risks the wrath of the heavens. The lightning doesn't always strike, but when it does, neither armour nor potions will help. Those who kill several villagers rarely have luck on their side.

What lurks in the world

  • Skeletons laugh at arrows. Meet them with a blade instead.
  • A burning creeper is about to burst. Keep your distance.
  • Deep below, the dark breeds monsters even close to your light.
  • Endermen can drag you to them through the void.
  • In the Nether the zombified piglins are always angry.
  • Never punch out a fire with your hands – the flames will catch you.
  • No jump towers: you can't build yourself upward by jumping and placing blocks beneath you. Build stairs or ladders.
  • After death you wake weak and hungry. Eat before you set out again.
  • The dragon in the End is far mightier here – and she returns.

Good to know

  • EXTRAHARD: monsters are stronger, falls hurt more and caves are dangerous. Never go down without light and food.
  • Torches fail in the deep: far down in the rock there isn't enough air for a torch. Bring lanterns when you go deeper.
  • Loose earth: dirt, grass and cobblestone won't hold just anything and can come crashing down. Build on solid ground.
  • Crowded pens: animals don't like being crowded and can die of the stress.
  • Stingy land: crops can wither, the desert bears nothing and snow kills the fields.
  • Protect your build with a golden shovel: right-click two opposite corners. Nobody else can build, break, or open your chests or doors there. To let a friend in, type /accesstrust name.
  • The night is dangerous: when darkness falls other players may turn on you, even at home. Walls and a closed door are what keep you safe, so don't forget to shut it behind you. Only the villages and spawn are always peaceful.
  • Nature heals: dropped saplings sometimes take root on their own.
